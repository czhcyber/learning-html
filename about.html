About

The person behind the keyboard

Short version: I am a student who likes problems that have a definite answer, and I am slowly getting comfortable with the ones that do not.

How I got here

My first website was a single page that said “hello” in a font I could not control. I remember being annoyed that the browser ignored what I had typed, and that annoyance is basically why I kept going. Learning that markup describes meaning and CSS describes appearance was the moment the whole thing clicked for me.

These days I study computer science and spend a lot of my free time on the web platform itself. I like that I can view source on almost anything and learn from it. I am not trying to be the fastest programmer in the room — I would rather understand one thing properly than half-understand five.

Outside of coursework I read a lot of non-fiction, I shoot 35 mm film on weekends, and I run slowly but regularly.

A short timeline

Four moments that shaped how I work.

  1. First “hello world”

    Edited a template until it broke, then spent an evening putting it back together. Learned that version control might be worth learning.

  2. Learning to inspect

    Discovered browser developer tools. Started reading other people’s CSS instead of guessing, which sped everything up enormously.

  3. Accessibility clicked

    Tried to use my own site with the keyboard only. It was unusable. I have written a skip link into every page since.

  4. Building this site

    Wrote it by hand, page by page, committing as I went, and published it with GitHub Pages so that other people could actually open it.
